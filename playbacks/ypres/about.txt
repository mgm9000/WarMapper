Positions come from the data pilot spreadsheet (215 Ypres rows). British and French lines on 21, 23, 25, 29 and 31 October and 4, 9 and 11 November are read from the sketch maps of the British official history (1925); each holds until the next sketch or the next change in the text. Everything else, and every German position, comes from text: the first four days of the official history, the German General Staff account of 1917, two British histories of 1916 and encyclopedia articles. No French source was read. Nothing here has been checked by a historian.
A line read from a sketch follows the drawn line to about 250 m. A line from text runs straight through the centres of the villages the sources name. The sketches show the Germans only as arrows, so German lines are text, moved where needed to stand 500 m clear of the Allied line. The sources give positions by day, taken here as nightfall.
Solid lines are positions a source gives. Dashed lines are inferred, interpolated or the last position on record; faded symbols are units the sources stop following.
Dark blue is British, light blue French, teal Belgian, red German. Zoomed out, German divisions fold into their corps; zoom in to see them.
The current map is a simplified drawing of today's roads, canals and villages from OpenStreetMap, with 1914 spellings in brackets when you zoom in. The woods and the old Ypres-Comines canal are approximate.
The 1914 map is Sketch A of the British official history (1925). It is bound across two pages and about a kilometre is lost in the fold, so the current map shows through the gap. Sketch of the day puts the official history's own sketch for the date under the units, with its British (red), French (blue) and German (green) lines; there are eight, from 18 October to 11 November. Both are placed to within about 150 m. Use the Units button to read the sketch on its own. On a sketch's last date the British and French units should sit on its solid line. The sketches also draw the lines of the days before; those are not in the data.
