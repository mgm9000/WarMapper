Unit positions come from the data pilot spreadsheet (38 rows) built from the Official Records, War Department tablets and Bachelder's map. Nothing here has been checked by a historian.
Solid symbols are positions a source gives. Dashed symbols are interpolated, inferred or the last position on record. A question mark means the location is a loose estimate.
Base map: John B. Bachelder, Map of the battle field of Gettysburg: First day's battle (1876), Boston Public Library copy. The whole sheet is shown; the ground of the first morning, west and north-west of the town, is drawn at twice the detail of the rest. Its printed red and blue bars show the whole day; the labelled symbols on top are this playback.
Current map: today's roads, streams, woods and names from OpenStreetMap, as far as the 1876 sheet reaches. North is up on both maps, so the 1876 sheet sits about 1° askew. At the three buildings used to fit the sheet the two maps differ by 15 to 18 metres. Road names are today's; the 1863 name follows in brackets where it differs. Satellite and terrain views are not in this prototype.
