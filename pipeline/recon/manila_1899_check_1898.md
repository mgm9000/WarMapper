# A check of the reconstructed lines against a second map (21 November 2026)

The lines of `data/recon/manila-1899.geojson` were read from the Army engineer map of February 1899 (S78). To see whether they hold
up against a map made by other surveyors, the traced shore, walls, Pasig and main roads were laid on Francisco J. de Gamoneda's
*Plano de Manila y sus Arrabales* (1898, 1:10,000; University of Texas copy, 1,280 px wide, about 6 m a pixel).

How. The plan was not fitted to the ground. Two landmarks that both maps draw as rings were matched: the Bilibid prison (engineer map
pixel 997.9, 1704.0; plan pixel 501.6, 289.3) and the Paco cemetery (1162.8, 2405.2; 935.3, 407.3). Those two points fix a turn, a
scale and a shift (5.94 m a plan pixel, the plan turned 63.5 degrees), and the traced lines were carried across by them.
With two points there is nothing left over to measure the match by, so the check is by eye.

What it shows.

- The walls of the Walled City, which were not used to place anything, fall on the plan's walls to a few plan pixels (10 to 20 m).
- The Pasig's outline follows the plan's river from the mouth to above San Miguel.
- The shore along Tondo and San Nicolas, and along Ermita and Malate, follows the plan's shore to about 20 to 30 m.
- Calle de Azcarraga, the road to Paco and the shore road lie on streets the plan draws.
- West of the Walled City the two cannot be compared: the plan draws the port then planned ("Puerto en proyecto"), not the shore.
  The engineer map's shore there runs through the planned basin, which fits harbour works begun but not finished.

What it does not show. The plan stops at Malate and Paco, so nothing south or east of them was checked; nor was anything north of
Tondo. No confidence level was raised on the strength of this: it was done by an AI model on a small copy, and a person should repeat it
on the full scan (University of Minnesota, umedia p16022coll205:757).
